Agent Protocols for Paid for Done

Which of today's agent protocols Span should use to talk to agents, prove who is paying, and move money, if developers are paid only for work the person accepts. Covers MPP, x401 and x402, then MCP and A2A, checked against what Span's code already does. Internal thinking, 24 October 2026.

Talk to tools

The short answer

No protocol knows what "accepted" means, and that is good for Span

I checked eight protocols. Not one has a step where the buyer says "I accept this work, release the money." Each leaves that decision to the merchant. MCP and A2A carry the work. x402 and MPP move the money the moment a request is paid. x401, AP2 and the card networks' agent programmes prove who authorised the payment.

So the acceptance record, the hold and the release have to live in Span. That is the part of "paid for done" that is hard to copy: Span is the merchant of record, it already reserves the person's money before work starts, and it already runs a quality check on every attempt. The protocols are pipes on either side of that.

In practice this means three choices, all small:

  1. Agents over A2A, with one Span extension that carries the quote, the delivery, the person's verdict and the payout notice. A2A's own "completed" state means delivered, never paid.
  2. Tools over MCP, priced per call as Span's own cost. Tools are inputs to a task; the outcome price is set at the task level.
  3. Money through rails Span already has. Prepaid credits with a budget hold on the buyer side, Stripe Connect on the developer side. x402 and MPP stay behind the existing wallet flags for paying outside per-call services, with x402's new hold-and-capture scheme as the one to pilot.

Moving money

The payment protocols

Stripe + Tempo · March 2026

MPP, the Machine Payments Protocol

What it is. An HTTP payment scheme filed with the IETF as an individual draft (draft-httpauth-payment-01, September 2026). A server answers 402 with WWW-Authenticate: Payment …; the client retries with Authorization: Payment …; the server returns a Payment-Receipt. It carries Tempo stablecoins, cards (Stripe shared payment tokens, Visa network tokens), Lightning, Solana and others, and runs over MCP too.

What fits. Sessions: the payer deposits into escrow, signs vouchers as work happens, and gets back whatever was never claimed. That is the nearest thing to "settle only what was accepted." Splits: one charge can pay up to eleven recipients at once on Tempo.

What doesn't. No hold-then-capture step, no refunds or disputes in the protocol, and on-chain charges cannot be reversed. Stripe's gas sponsorship cannot be used with Connect, which is how Span pays developers. Stripe's API for it is still in preview.

For Span. Already wired by decision record 027. Useful for a headless agent that wants to become a Span customer with no card, and for paying outside services priced in MPP. The eleven-way split would make developers direct payees, which works against Span holding the money, so don't use it for payouts.

Sources: stripe.com/blog/machine-payments-protocol · datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-httpauth-payment · mpp.dev

Proof.com · June 2026

x401: proving who is paying

What it is. An identity protocol, not a payment one. A server answers 401 with a PROOF-REQUEST header saying which credentials it accepts (built on OpenID4VP); the agent retries with PROOF-RESPONSE carrying verifiable credentials (SD-JWT, mDoc). Its own tagline: "x402 lets agents pay. x401 lets agents prove who's paying." Draft 0.2.0, with contributors from Circle, Okta, OpenAI and Visa.

Maturity. A Cloudflare Workers package and a demo at Money20/20 on 19 October (prove with x401, pay with x402 from a Circle wallet). I found no named production user. The spec lists "agent authentication" as unsolved: the proof is bound to the server, not to the agent presenting it.

For Span. Low priority. Span already identifies developer agents with signed AgentFacts and identifies people with its own accounts. x401 becomes useful if a business's buyer agent must prove it may spend for its company (a business customer paying by agent). Revisit when it has production users.

Sources: x401.id · github.com/proof/x401 · proof.com/blog/introducing-x401

Coinbase → x402 Foundation · v2

x402, the baseline, and its new hold-and-capture scheme

What it is. The HTTP 402 protocol most agent payments use: PAYMENT-REQUIRED, then PAYMENT-SIGNATURE, then PAYMENT-RESPONSE, settled in stablecoin by a facilitator. Version 2 since December 2025. Governed by a Linux Foundation body since July 2026 whose members include Stripe, Visa, Mastercard, Google, AWS and Shopify.

The important part for us: auth-capture (a draft, v1.1 of 18 August 2026). It places a hold, lets the merchant capture all or part of it, void it or refund it, and gives the money back to the payer automatically if nobody captures by a deadline. On capture, a fee within limits the payer signed goes to one fee receiver and the rest to the payee. That is the closest any protocol comes to "hold, then release on acceptance," and the automatic return is a built-in promise to the buyer.

What to watch. The plain exact scheme is final on payment, with no chargebacks. I could not confirm that Coinbase's facilitator settles auth-capture today. One newer scheme was pulled from Coinbase's SDK after it was found able to accept a buyer's payment without paying the receiver. Real volume is small: an analyst measured about $28,000 a day in March, roughly half of it wash trading.

For Span. Pilot auth-capture on test networks for stablecoin buyers: Span is the one allowed to capture, the deadline is the work time plus 72 hours, and the payee is Span itself, with developers credited in Span's ledger. Don't depend on it until a facilitator settles it in production.

Sources: github.com/x402-foundation/x402 (v2 spec, auth-capture scheme) · linuxfoundation.org x402 Foundation release · docs.x402r.org

Carrying the work

MCP and A2A

Spec 2026-07-28 · Agentic AI Foundation

MCP: for tools, not for hiring agents

Where it is now. The July 2026 revision is the largest since launch. It drops the session handshake, so every request stands alone. It moves long-running "tasks" into an official extension, with the states working, input required, completed, cancelled and failed. It deprecates sampling, so a server can no longer borrow the caller's model. Login is OAuth 2.1. MCP has been run by the Agentic AI Foundation, under the Linux Foundation, since December 2025. The registry is still a preview.

Payments. None in the spec. The formal proposal to add x402 payments (SEP-2007) was closed in June 2026 because no maintainer sponsored it. Paid MCP tools exist outside the spec (Cloudflare, Vercel, an MPP binding draft), and the foundation's own advice is to charge at a gateway in front of the server.

Limits for us. MCP is a client calling a tool. It has no agent identity card, no peer discovery and no step where the client accepts a result. A tool that returns an error still counts as "completed."

For Span. Keep MCP for tools, as Span does now, and treat each tool call as Span's own cost, metered per call. Don't build the hiring of developer agents on it. If Span ever publishes a public MCP server, the price it shows should be the outcome price of the task, not a per-call price.

Sources: modelcontextprotocol.io specification and changelog (2026-07-28) · tasks.extensions.modelcontextprotocol.io · github.com/modelcontextprotocol PR #2007

v1.0 · Agentic AI Foundation since August 2026

A2A: for hiring agents, with one gap

Where it is now. Version 1.0 runs over JSON-RPC, gRPC or plain HTTP. It has task states (submitted, working, input required, auth required, completed, failed, cancelled, rejected), results delivered as artifacts, streaming, push notifications to a webhook, optionally signed Agent Cards, and extensions declared on the card. It joined MCP in the same foundation in August 2026. IBM's competing protocol merged into it, and Cisco's AGNTCY project dropped its own in its favour. Over 150 organisations use it, mostly large vendors.

The gap. No price, quote, acceptance or dispute field. "Completed" is final: a send-back has to be a new task that points to the old one. The official x402 extension (v0.1, over a year old) asks for payment while the task waits for input and settles immediately. That is "pay first," the opposite of what we want.

For Span. A2A is the right base for developer agents, and the engine already speaks it. Make it the recommended format for new developers, keep Span's own format for the ones already on it, and add one extension.

Sources: a2a-protocol.org/latest/specification · a2a-protocol.org life-of-a-task · github.com/google-agentic-commerce/a2a-x402

How the four Span moments travel

MomentA2A todayWhat Span adds
1. Price quote before workNothing binding. The task can wait for input, but there is no quote shape.quote: amount, currency, expiry, the hold, the 72-hour window. Sent by Span; the agent agrees or declines before work starts.
2. Work deliverednative Artifact, then completed.Span reads "completed" as delivered, waiting for acceptance.
3. The person accepts or sends backNo verb. A send-back is a new task referring to the old one.verdict: accepted, sent back with the reason, or disputed, pointing to the original task and attempt.
4. Money releasedOnly x402's "paid now" receipt.payout: held, released or clawed back, sent to the developer's webhook. The money moves through Stripe Connect.

All four ride in A2A's standard extension metadata, so any A2A agent can read them without new transport. The extension is marked required on Span's card, so an agent that doesn't understand it is told so at the start, before work, rather than surprised at payout.

Putting it together

One picture: pipes on both sides, the decision in the middle

  PERSON (or their agent)                 SPAN                         DEVELOPER AGENT
  pays with credits, card,         quote, hold, Judge,               A2A + Span outcome
  or wallet (x402 / MPP)    ──▶    person's verdict,        ──▶      extension
                                   72-hour window,
                                   release or void
  hold placed on their money      the acceptance record             earnings held, then paid
                                         │                          by Stripe Connect
                                         ▼
                                   TOOLS over MCP
                                   per-call cost, Span's own

Buyer side. For most people the hold already exists: prepaid credits and the budget hold placed before dispatch. Credits have one real advantage over a card hold: no card network deadline. A card hold on a payment the merchant starts can expire after 4 days and 18 hours (Visa), which leaves under two days for the work before a 72-hour window begins. For cards, keep charging into credits up front and run the hold in Span's ledger. For stablecoin buyers, the wallet in decision record 027 already does a hold, and x402's auth-capture is the protocol version of it.

Developer side. The one thing to build. Earnings land as held, then become payable on acceptance or after 72 hours of silence, or are voided on a send-back. Stripe Connect transfers stay as they are. Splits are Span's ledger, never the protocol: Span is the merchant of record and holds the money, so developers are never direct payees on a chain.

Evidence. Later, an AP2-style mandate ("up to $X if I accept within 72 hours"), signed by the person, would make every release defensible in a dispute. Not needed to start.

Your call

Four things to decide

1

Make A2A the recommended way for developers to connect?

The engine already speaks it; the decision is to say so in a decision record, make it the SDK default for new agents, and keep Span's own format working for existing ones.

My recommendation: yes. It is where the field settled (IBM and Cisco folded their protocols into it), it shares a foundation with MCP, and it costs Span nothing new.

2

Write the Span outcome extension?

Quote, verdict and payout messages on top of A2A, published so developers can build against it.

My recommendation: yes, after the shadow report has run. The report tells us what "accepted" should mean; the extension then writes it down.

3

Hold developer earnings until acceptance?

The core change behind paid for done. It touches what developers are paid and when.

My recommendation: yes, in shadow first. Mark earnings held in the ledger without delaying payouts, and compare for a few weeks.

4

Pilot x402 auth-capture?

On test networks, behind the existing wallet flags, for buyers paying in stablecoin.

My recommendation: watch, don't build yet. It is a draft and no facilitator is confirmed to settle it. Revisit when one does. MPP and x401 stay where they are.

What I couldn't confirm

Open questions

  • Whether Coinbase's facilitator settles x402 auth-capture in production.
  • Whether Stripe's shared payment tokens (used by ACP and MPP) allow a hold with later capture.
  • Whether MPP's Visa card method supports holds.
  • AP2 version 0.2's exact mandate names: confirmed on Google's blog, not read in the spec itself.
  • Adoption numbers for MPP ("$14M in 60 days") and x401 (two unnamed pilots) come from secondary reports.
  • I could not watch any of the launch videos; everything here is from specs, repositories and written announcements.
