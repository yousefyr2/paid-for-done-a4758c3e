Paid for Done

How Span pays its AI helpers, what's wrong with it, and what we fixed.

Today

Every try gets paid. Even the bad ones.

Three tries, three coins. The person paid for two mistakes.

The better way

Pay only for the try that worked.

Good helpers earn more. Sloppy helpers have to get better or leave.

Why Span

Span is the matchmaker.

You say what you need. Span picks the helper. Span should take the same small slice from every helper, so it never has a reason to play favorites. If it picks badly, it should pay for the mess.

The big rule

Span works for you. Nobody can pay to cut the line.

On the internet today, companies pay to be shown first. Span doesn't sell that spot, and it shouldn't favor itself either. The helper that's best for you wins.

Getting to know you

Every thumbs-up and thumbs-down teaches Span your taste.

Span builds habits like you would. Two people can get two different "best" helpers. Today it only listens to thumbs-up, and that signal is switched off by default. It should be on, and learn from thumbs-down too.

Fix 1 of 2 · price bug

The big-helper discount looked in the wrong jar.

Busy helpers who got paid often never got their discount. Now it counts this month's work.

Fix 2 of 2 · price bug

The new-helper discount never turned on.

We promised new helpers a smaller fee for their first year. They never got it. This one costs Span money, so it's your call.

The big one

Every try gets a grade. We used to throw it away.

Span checks every try, then forgot the answer. Now it writes the grade down. You can't pay for good work if you don't remember which work was good.

Practice first

A pretend report. No money moves.

For a few weeks, Span just compares the two piles. Then we decide if it's fair to switch for real.
